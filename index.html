About

A commitment to useful public service.

Growing up, I watched people I loved navigate economic hardship and institutional barriers that narrowed their choices. Those experiences made the stakes of public service personal and drew me toward a question that still guides my work: how can institutions become more responsive to the people who depend on them?

Caregiving at The Special Angels of The Woodlands during high school and undergrad gave that question an everyday form. Being present, paying attention, and building relationships showed me how much the experience of support depends on the person providing it. My studies in government and economics helped me examine the systems behind those encounters; my work in classrooms, campaigns, and reentry programs has kept the people within them at the center.

I want to bring those perspectives together in a career in public service and, over time, public leadership. For me, that means understanding why institutions fall short, listening to the people affected, and doing the practical work of making them serve people better.
