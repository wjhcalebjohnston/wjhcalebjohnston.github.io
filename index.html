Why public service

Understanding institutions before trying to lead them.

Growing up, I saw how addiction, economic hardship, justice-system involvement, and institutional indifference can narrow people’s choices. That experience did not give me easy answers. It gave me a responsibility to learn how public systems actually work before claiming I could improve them.

That is why my path crosses research, reentry services, teaching, campaign operations, and political data. I am interested in the gap between what a policy promises and what a person encounters: the handoff that fails, the rule no one explains, the dataset that hides a lived reality, or the procedure that quietly restores dignity.

I completed all requirements for Georgetown University’s M.A. in American Government; degree conferral is expected in August 2026. I earned a B.A. in Economics and Government from the University of Texas at Austin in 2024.

A guiding idea

Institutions are experienced at the front desk.

The quality of a public system is often revealed in its ordinary moments: whether someone receives a clear answer, a respectful handoff, and a path forward.

Beyond the work

Preparing for a career of public responsibility.

I do not see public service as a title to claim early. I see it as a practice: listening carefully, doing the unglamorous operational work, testing assumptions against evidence, and taking responsibility for what happens after a recommendation leaves the page.

The common thread across my work is a desire to earn larger responsibility by first becoming dependable in smaller moments. Outside work, I recharge with dogs, basketball, and the friends and family who keep ambition grounded.
